Blockchain

2017

Source Blockchain, a Design Issues note by Tim Berners-Lee, 2017-10-31. read the original

The question

Volume VIII closes with a note from October 2017, written at the height of an enthusiasm, and its opening move is to refuse the question:

What technical problem is there in the world in 2017 where someone has not asked "Will the blockchain solve that?". The response is then typically "Well, what do you mean by blockchain exactly?", as it really depends which aspect of which system you are talking about. Blockchain and the Web, 2017

Berners-Lee is careful about his standing: he makes no attempt to explain the technology, about which he says he is not an expert. What he does is separate the things being named by one word, which is the method of Chapter 25 applied to a different word.

81.1 Five things in one system

81.2 Two ironies

The note records two, and calls them that. The first is about openness:

The ledger system works and maintains its integrity only because of the complete openness of the blocks in the chain, and the fact that many systems were constantly checking it; and yet bitcoin in fact provided a way that value could be used anonymously for criminal purposes with impunity. Blockchain and the Web, 2017

Total transparency of the record, used to obtain total opacity of the participants.

The second is about cost:

Also, for a new fast and light system of transfer of value, it was ironic that the mining needed to both generate new coin and maintain the verified integrity of the chain necessarily by design involves burning huge amounts of energy, which is not ecologically sustainable. Blockchain and the Web, 2017

A lightweight replacement for heavy institutions, whose integrity is purchased with electricity, by design.

81.3 The warning about encrypting in public

One paragraph makes a point that applies far beyond this subject and is rarely stated so plainly:

In general the practice of encrypting stuff and making the encrypted version public requires faith that the encryption method don't be cracked in any timescale which is important to the parties. Blockchain and the Web, 2017

Publishing an encrypted thing is a bet about the future. The cost of breaking it falls over time, with faster machines, discovered weaknesses and new techniques, while the published copy stays available to everyone forever. A system that records permanently is a system that must assume its encryption is eventually broken.

81.4 Speculation, and an old chart

The section on value opens with an observation about what the price is for, and it is unsentimental:

To first order, for lots of operations transferring money between different currencies and countries, the value (say in USD) of a bitcoin is irrelevant. For those making a remittance, say of USD from the USA to (say) Haiti, all one need to do is own some Bitcoin for a minute between buying them in the US and selling them in Haiti. Blockchain and the Web, 2017

For the use he considers most valuable, moving money to somewhere it is hard to move money, the price does not matter, because nobody holds it. And then the note places, beside a chart of the rise in value, a chart of a technology stock index up to 1999, with a remark about people buying because of the prices they imagine other people will pay. No conclusion is drawn. The juxtaposition is the comment.

81.5 Why this closes the volume

Volume VIII has been about the places where the Web’s architecture meets institutions: courts, regulators, registries, copyright. Every chapter has described a mechanism whose limits are social rather than technical.

A distributed ledger is the proposal to replace the institution with a mechanism, and the note is a careful refusal to accept that framing. It grants the parts that are genuinely new, notes the ways they connect to what exists, and observes that the integrity everyone admires is purchased in a currency that has to come from somewhere.

Its most useful contribution is the first paragraph’s demand: say which thing you mean. A word that names a ledger, a currency, a naming system, an identity system and a movement cannot be evaluated as one thing, and most argument about it is people answering different questions.

What happened since

  • The energy objection was answered by one network and not the other. Ethereum replaced mining with staking in September 2022, cutting its consumption by around 99 per cent; the original network did not.
  • Identity took the route the note names. Decentralised identifiers became a W3C Recommendation in July 2022, giving people identifiers that do not depend on the domain name system, which is one of the five parts of Figure 81.1 arriving as a standard.
  • Payments arrived in the browser, generically. The Payment Request specification became a Recommendation in September 2022, providing the modular slot this note describes, without favouring any currency.
  • The speculation chart resolved. The price rose far beyond 2017 levels and fell sharply more than once, and the remittance use the note considers the most valuable remains marginal relative to trading.

Questions 4

81.1 Take a question of the form “will this solve that?” from your own field. Separate the several things the word names.

81.2 Publishing an encrypted thing is a bet about the future. For a system you run, how long does the bet have to hold?

81.3 The ledger and the currency depend on each other. Design a shared ledger whose integrity does not require a currency, and say what it costs.

81.4 The price is irrelevant to the remittance use. Explain why, and say what would make it relevant again.

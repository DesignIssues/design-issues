Notation3

2000

Source Notation3, a Design Issues note by Tim Berners-Lee, 2000-10-01. read the original

The question

This is the specification of the notation Berners-Lee designed to write the logic of Chapter 40 down. It is the most technical note in the collection and the only one that reads as a paper, with formal syntax, a definition of entailment, and a catalogue of built-in operators.

The design question it answers is not what logic is true, but what logic survives being published.

42.1 Written for an open world

Whereas a closed system may be built based on a single knowledge base of believed facts, an open web-based system exists in an unbounded sea of interconnected information resources. This requires that an agent be aware of the provenance of information, and responsible for its disposition. Notation 3 Logic, 2000

From which the first requirement follows immediately. To say what a document said, one must be able to quote a set of statements and match it against a pattern. Quotation is therefore not an advanced feature but a starting condition, which is why Chapter 30 puts it in the base layer.

42.2 The trap that shaped the design

Quotation plus reference brings self-reference, and self-reference brings paradox. Berners-Lee’s response is unusually direct about the cost:

This quotation and reference, with its inevitable possibility of direct or indirect self-reference, if added directly to first order logic presents problems such as paradox traps. To avoid this, N3 logic has deliberately been kept to limited expressive power: it currently contains no general first order negation. Notation 3 Logic, 2000

A language built to express anything is deliberately made unable to express general negation, because the alternative is a system that can tie itself in knots. Negated forms of particular built-in operations are provided instead.

42.3 Monotonicity as a publishing requirement

The second design goal is stated as a claim about other people rather than about logic:

Further, we require that other users of N3 in the web can express new knowledge without affecting systems we have already built. Notation 3 Logic, 2000

New information may contradict old information, which must then be resolved. What it must not do is silently change what the old information meant. That is monotonicity, and Berners-Lee identifies exactly where non-monotonicity comes from in existing systems: a form of negation that treats a sentence as false when it is not derivable from the current knowledge base. The culprit is the phrase “current knowledge base”, which is a variable quantity that can be referred to implicitly.

42.4 Scoped negation as failure

The solution is to keep the useful behaviour and remove the implicit reference. Negation is permitted only about a specific named document or formula: whether that document holds, or allows derivation of, a given fact.

42.5 The Web as an operator

Two built-in properties do something no logic textbook contains: they make retrieval part of inference. One relates a document to what it means, and the other to what it says. Berners-Lee defines the semantics of a document as the result of retrieving a representation, parsing it, and assimilating it into the local framework.

A reviewer was aghast at defining semantics that way, and the footnote answering the objection is the wittiest passage in the collection:

I suspect however that the meaning of the paper to the reviewer could be considered quite equivalently the result of the process of retrieval of a representation of the paper, its parsing by the review, and its assimilation in terms of the reviewer's local logical framework: a similar though perhaps imperfect process. Notation 3 Logic, 2000

42.6 What the separation bought

The conclusion records an outcome that the note’s own structure made possible:

The separation between the Notation3 extensions to RDF and the logic properties has allowed N3 by itself to be used in many other applications directly, and to be used with other properties to provide other functionality such as the expression of patches (updates) Notation 3 Logic, 2000

The notation and the logic are separable, so the notation can be used by people who want none of the logic. That is the modularity of Chapter 19, applied by the same author to his own design, and it is why the readable half of this work outlived the rest.

What happened since

  • The notation won; the logic did not. Turtle, which is the data half of this notation with the logic removed, became a W3C Recommendation in February 2014 and is now the ordinary way people read and write graph data. The rules, formulae and built-ins remained a research language.
  • Scoped negation stayed a minority position. The distinction of Figure 42.2 is correct and is rarely offered by the systems people actually use, most of which treat absence from their own store as falsehood.
  • Quoting graphs was standardised twice over. Named graphs arrived with SPARQL in 2008, and RDF-star work from 2021 onward added statements about statements, which is the requirement section 42.1 opens with.
  • Retrieval as inference did not survive. Making a network fetch part of evaluating a rule proved difficult to reason about, and production systems load data first and reason afterwards.

Questions 4

42.1 The notation omits general negation to avoid paradox. Write a statement you cannot make without it, and say whether a scoped form would serve.

42.2 Monotonicity is required so that others may publish without disturbing your system. Name a system where that requirement does not hold, and what it costs users.

42.3 Defining the meaning of a document as retrieve, parse, assimilate makes meaning depend on the network. Give two things that can go wrong.

42.4 The data half of the notation outlived the logic half. Was that separation luck or design? Argue from section 42.6.

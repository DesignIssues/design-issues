Chapter 74

The Intimacy Gradient

2023

Source The Intimacy Gradient, a Design Issues note by Tim Berners-Lee, 2023-04-14. read the original

The question

Volume VII closes with a note from 2022 that takes its central idea from architecture, in the ordinary sense, and applies it to systems that have only ever offered two settings.

If in our systems we cater for only the public and only the private extremes, we miss out most of our lives! Most of our lives involve doing things with combinations of different sized groups of people. The Intimacy Gradient, 2022

74.1 The building

The image is worked carefully before it is applied:

A city has public places where I can do all kinds of things, and also a private house with a private room which may be by myself. In that house there are spaces where I do things with family, friends, colleagues. The Intimacy Gradient, 2022

The requirement this produces is threefold, and each part is about the person rather than the mechanism: that I can easily recognise the difference, easily know which I am in, and easily welcome people into the more intimate areas.

74.2 The mechanism he proposes is colour

The practical suggestion is almost disarmingly small:

A simple way to start making an intimacy gradient on the web is with style. The Intimacy Gradient, 2022

His own organisation’s site had used three background colours for three levels of access for decades, and the reason given is about safety rather than aesthetics:

For one thing, for security, anyone looking at a page will know who they can and cannot share things with. The Intimacy Gradient, 2022

The convention of putting a warning word in the name of public mailing lists is recorded as a second measure, because the first was not enough. And then the observation that names the gap in every tool anyone uses:

Mail clients don't color the background of a message you are composing as a function of how large the audience is your are addressing - bit perhaps they could! The Intimacy Gradient, 2022

The mistake this would prevent is one almost everyone has made, and no widely used tool prevents it.

74.3 Both ends of the gradient deserve design

The note does something unusual with its own proposal in the last third. Having argued for letting people style their public profile, because people want to be proud of it and direct others to it, Berners-Lee turns the argument around:

However, thinking about both intimacy gradients and the different styles of the W3C web site, surely we should not stop there but make sure we think about the style of the private places too. The Intimacy Gradient, 2022

Personal expression has been thought about only at the public end, because that is the end other people see. The private end is where a person spends most of their time, and it has been left to look like whatever the software looks like.

The instruction he gives is two-sided, and it is the general form of the whole chapter: make the default experience in intimate places one that respects that intimacy, and also let people control it.

74.4 Why this closes the volume

Every chapter in Volume VII has been about a system that models a social arrangement too narrowly. Trust is modelled as one hierarchy. Security is modelled as a boundary. A party is modelled as a domain. Identity is modelled as a password. Sharing is modelled as a switch.

This chapter names what all of them are missing, and the name comes from someone who was designing buildings rather than protocols. People do not live at the ends of a scale. They live in the rooms between, and a system with two settings makes every visit a little awkward.

What happened since

  • Audience selection stayed coarse. Most platforms still offer a small fixed set of audiences, which is the three-option model of Chapter 64, and overlapping groups of different sizes remain hard to express.
  • Colour as a safety cue spread partially. Some collaboration tools now mark external or public channels visibly, which is section 74.2’s proposal adopted where the cost of a mistake is commercial.
  • The composing window is still uncoloured. No widely used mail client varies its appearance with the size of the audience, and sending to the wrong list remains an ordinary accident.
  • The pattern language kept being borrowed. Alexander’s patterns continued to be applied to software, and this is among the more literal applications: a gradient of rooms, for data.

Chapter 72

Inference over Private Data

2023

Source Inference over Private Data, a Design Issues note by Tim Berners-Lee, 2023. read the original

The short version

In September 2023 Google let its chatbot Bard read people’s Gmail. A journalist, Kevin Roose, tried it on his own mail. Berners-Lee suspects it did not gain the insight a person would: it had been shown the mail, never trained on mail. Only a giant platform or an autocratic state could train on everyone’s private data; a better route is for groups with a shared reason to pool data under rules they trust.

The question

Written in November 2023, the note opens with an epigraph by a machine, Inflection’s chatbot Pi: “Ah, the holy grail of AI! Private data pods are a really hot topic right now”. Those pods are the idea behind Solid (Chapter 54). The note tests whether the easy promise holds: what does an assistant working for its user need beyond the user’s data?

72.1 One packaging, assumed for all AI

The launch of ChatGPT by OpenAI in November 2022 made huge numbers of people aware of large language models (LLMs): programs trained to continue text plausibly.

As a result, there was a tendency on the ensuing months to assume that any powerful AI would be packaged and exposed in the same way. Inference from Private Data, 2023

The assumed shapes were three: an image generator, a text generator and a chatbot. In each, text went to the model and an answer came back. Who benefits was set by that packaging, not by the technology. Users were warned that anything confidential might be reused by the company.

72.2 Rewiring, not rebuilding

What is wrong is not the model but the wiring:

From the point of view of privacy and user empowerment, to be a beneficent app, an improved wiring of the LLM would involve all of the insights and benefit being available to the user themselves. Inference from Private Data, 2023

Pi, from Inflection, “provides this wiring, it seems”. If so, it could be “a personal helper, assistant, maybe therapist, that you can trust”. Over a long conversation he found it seemed to understand him better.

72.3 Shown is not trained

A language model learns by being trained: training adjusts the model itself, over millions of examples, until it has absorbed how that kind of text works. Showing Roose’s mail to Bard only placed it in front of the finished model, one question at a time.

Supplying that wisdom means training on many people’s private mail, which an assistant like “Charlie, the AI which works for you” (Chapters 93 and 94) would need. Few could get it:

Well, it isn't unless you are a very large platform willing to train on all its users, or a state-run system in an autocratic state. Inference from Private Data, 2023

That leaves the note’s question:

For public things, it had wisdom about how they all connect. For private email, no wisdom. How could another re-wiring give us that wisdom? Inference from Private Data, 2023

72.4 The proposal: groups rather than everybody

One way to proceed is, rather than doing inference over the whole private web, to do it over a subset, and specifically a group of people who have something in common which gives them a specific inventive to share their data to a achieve greater insight. Inference from Private Data, 2023

The classic case is patients with one disease pooling data to learn about treatments or discover drugs. It needs a social construction that raises trust enough for people to contribute: what “have been called Data Trusts or, more recently, Data Institutions”.

72.5 Why the assistant pulls a user into one provider

Of Bard, the note warns:

Obviously this is going to work most easily when you have a lot of personal data with the same provider and the same provider as your AI, and so the risk of further monopolization and data siloing is increased. Inference from Private Data, 2023

The silo that Chapter 54 set out to break gains one more pull.

72.6 Computing on data nobody sees

An Oxford Martin School EWADA project lets gig-economy workers such as Uber drivers learn group facts like average pay through multi-party computation (MPC): a result computed from private inputs without revealing them.

In the simplest form (an illustration, not EWADA’s design), three drivers each split their weekly pay into three random numbers that sum to it, and give one to each of three nodes. Each node publishes only the sum of what it holds. Those sums add up to the total pay, and so the average, yet no node saw a real figure. The limit:

MPC is not the sort of thing which will scale to training an LLM, but it can have a role to play in achieving group insight from personal data. Inference from Private Data, 2023

72.7 Consent, and the conclusion

Most of these arrangements track each person’s consent to their data being used for the group’s good. The note concludes that assistants must both access and understand private data, through combinations of three things:

IngredientExample in this chapter
Respecting personal dataPi: meant to give the insight to the user
Using personal dataBard reading a user’s Gmail
Training on a lot or a little of itPatients pooling data through a data trust

Policy and law must supply protection the data architecture cannot give itself. Solid makes each combination easier, the note says, with standard consent management. It ends on the stakes:

The goals in sight, such as medical solutions for group of patients, and powerful agents which significantly empower a human being, are very exciting. Inference from Private Data, 2023

What happened since

  • Assistants stayed wired to the platforms holding the data (2024). Bard, renamed Gemini in February 2024, kept its links to Gmail and Drive, as 72.5 predicts.
  • The shown-against-trained gap narrowed, but did not close (2024). Gemini 1.5 (February 2024) could be shown about a million tokens (word-pieces) at once, a whole mailbox: still showing, not training.
  • Pi’s makers moved on (2024). In March 2024 Inflection’s co-founders Mustafa Suleyman and Karén Simonyan joined Microsoft to run a new consumer AI group, and Inflection turned to building models for business customers.

Chapter 72

Inference over Private Data

2023

Source Inference over Private Data, a Design Issues note by Tim Berners-Lee, 2023. read the original

The short version

A language model learns by being trained on huge amounts of text; inference means drawing conclusions from data. Private mail was not part of that training, so an assistant can read a person’s mail without understanding mail. Only a giant platform or a state could train on everyone’s private data. A better route is for groups with a shared reason to pool their data, under rules they trust.

The question

Written in November 2023, the note opens with an epigraph produced by a machine: a passage from Inflection’s chatbot Pi praising private data “pods”. Berners-Lee then examines whether what it promises is achievable. What would an assistant that genuinely works for its user need, and why can it not simply be given the user’s data?

72.1 What the launch changed

The launch of ChatGPT by OpenAI in November 2022 made a huge number of people aware, for the first time, of large language models (LLMs): programs trained to continue text plausibly.

As a result, there was a tendency on the ensuing months to assume that any powerful AI would be packaged and exposed in the same way. Inference from Private Data, 2023

The assumed shapes were an image generator, a text generator and a chatbot. In each a person types and the system answers; who benefits is fixed by the packaging, not the technology. People were warned not to discuss confidential things with such systems, since what they shared might be used by the company running them.

72.2 Rewiring, not rebuilding

What is wrong is not the model but the wiring:

From the point of view of privacy and user empowerment, to be a beneficent app, an improved wiring of the LLM would involve all of the insights and benefit being available to the user themselves. Inference from Private Data, 2023

His example of the improved wiring is Pi, which he tried and found seemed to take on a better understanding of him over a long conversation. Chapter 92 gives the general form of this argument.

72.3 Shown is not trained

In September 2023 Google let its chatbot Bard read a user’s Gmail. When the journalist Kevin Roose tried it on his own mail, Berners-Lee suspects it did not gain the dramatic insight a person would have. His tentative answer to why is the heart of the note: it had been shown the email but not trained on email.

Training adjusts the model itself, over millions of examples, until it has absorbed how that kind of text works. Being shown something only places it in front of the finished model for one question.

Supplying that wisdom would mean training on many people’s private mail, and Berners-Lee says who could do it:

Well, it isn't unless you are a very large platform willing to train on all its users, or a state-run system in an autocratic state. Inference from Private Data, 2023

Both are the parties one would least want doing it.

72.4 The proposal: groups rather than everybody

One way to proceed is, rather than doing inference over the whole private web, to do it over a subset, and specifically a group of people who have something in common which gives them a specific inventive to share their data to a achieve greater insight. Inference from Private Data, 2023

(The slips “inventive” and “a achieve” are in the original.) The classic case is patients with the same disease pooling data to learn about treatments, and even to discover new drugs. This needs a social construction that raises trust enough for individuals to contribute; such arrangements “have been called Data Trusts or, more recently, Data Institutions”. Chapter 66’s account of modelling real trust is what this needs, and Chapter 22 shows how such a group would agree its terms.

72.5 The warning about siloing

Of the Bard announcement, the note adds a warning:

Obviously this is going to work most easily when you have a lot of personal data with the same provider and the same provider as your AI, and so the risk of further monopolization and data siloing is increased. Inference from Private Data, 2023

The silo of Chapter 54, a provider whose stored data works only with its own apps, acquires a new and much stronger reason to exist.

72.6 Computing on data nobody sees

A project in the Oxford Martin School’s EWADA group lets gig-economy workers, such as Uber drivers, learn group facts like average pay without revealing their own figures. It uses multi-party computation (MPC), in which a result is computed from private inputs without disclosing the inputs, spread over a federated network of MPC nodes. The limit is stated plainly:

MPC is not the sort of thing which will scale to training an LLM, but it can have a role to play in achieving group insight from personal data. Inference from Private Data, 2023

72.7 Consent, and the conclusion

Common to most of these arrangements is tracking each person’s consent to their data being used for the group’s good. The note concludes that we will need assistants that both access and understand private data, built from combinations of three things:

IngredientExample in this chapter
Respecting personal dataPi: the insight goes to the user alone
Using personal dataBard reading a user’s Gmail
Training on a lot or a little of itPatients pooling data through a data trust

Policy and law must supply protection the data architecture cannot give itself. Solid makes each combination easier, the note says, coupled with standard consent management: a record of who agreed to which use of their data.

What happened since

  • Assistants stayed wired to the platforms holding the data (2024). Bard, renamed Gemini in February 2024, kept its links to Gmail and Drive: the concentration section 72.5 predicts.
  • The shown-against-trained gap narrowed, but did not close (2024). Google’s Gemini 1.5, announced in February 2024, could be shown around a million tokens (word-pieces) at once, so a whole mailbox fits in front of the model. That is still showing, not the training Figure 72.2 describes.
  • Older techniques offer another route. Federated learning (proposed in 2016), which trains on devices so raw data stays put, and MPC (dating from the early 1980s) are attempts to obtain the wisdom of section 72.3 without collecting the corpus.

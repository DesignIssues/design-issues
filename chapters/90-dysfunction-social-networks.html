Chapter 90

The Dysfunction of Social Networks

2024

Source The Dysfunction of Social Networks, a Design Issues note by Tim Berners-Lee, 2024. read the original

The short version

Social networks earn money from data about each user, so that advertisers can target them. Separately, their feeds learn to show whatever keeps people scrolling, which is often false or enraging. This 2024 note separates the harmful steps from the harmless ones and lists who can fix them.

The question

During the 2016 US election, young people in Macedonia made money from websites funded by Google advertisements. Of the headlines they tried, the best earner, the note says, was “Hilary really wants Trump to win” (the note’s spelling). None had a stake in the election; the advertising system had taught them what paid. In 2024 Berners-Lee turned the collection’s method on the thing he built:

After writing a lot about how the Web works, it is not unreasonable to write about how it is not working. The Dysfunction of Social Networks, 2024

He narrows the subject:

Here we focus on arguably the deepest harm to society, the mental health of children and teens, and the rise of hatred, polarization of opinion, and threats to democracy. The Dysfunction of Social Networks, 2024

90.1 A wise, warm, self-governing world?

In the early days of the web, it seemed that the world of humanity connected by technology could become a wise, warm, self-governing one. The Dysfunction of Social Networks, 2024

He calls John Perry Barlow’s 1996 Declaration of the Independence of Cyberspace a naive utopian vision, though the early years had much that was good. Then:

After a few years, people joined the social networks instead of making websites,and the social networks became monopolies. The Dysfunction of Social Networks, 2024

90.2 From data to manipulation

The 2018 Facebook–Cambridge Analytica scandal showed that people had been manipulated by targeted advertising built from their social media profiles. What the public then feared went further:

Rather than one worrying about the exposure of their own personal data, they also worried about the indirect effect it was having on other people. The Dysfunction of Social Networks, 2024

And those targeted cannot see it:

An insidious aspect is that the targeted individual are not aware the fact that other groups may be shown wildly different and inconsistent versions. The Dysfunction of Social Networks, 2024

A profile is the picture a service keeps of a user; targeting is choosing each person’s advertisements by it. The note’s chain:

Retention and correlation

The chain starts with what the note calls spying, broadly:

Here we use "Spying" for any retention of user data. The Dysfunction of Social Networks, 2024

Some the user knows about, such as forms and photos; some not, such as their IP address, location and super-cookie parameters (hidden identifiers that survive clearing cookies).

Correlation means concluding that two records describe the same person, which the note calls “the dark art”:

It includes things like browser type, processor speed, typing timing, and so on, which together make a characteristic signature for a person which will be consistent between different sites. The Dysfunction of Social Networks, 2024

Such a signature is called a fingerprint. Chapter 71 argued that what a person reads should not be watched; fingerprinting lets strangers watch it anyway.

Where the harm begins

The note grants targeting its benefits, more effective ads for advertisers and more relevant ones for users, and accepts that applications need profiles:

So the profile building itself is not the problem. The problems are when that profile is used to target the user in a way as to take advantage of them The Dysfunction of Social Networks, 2024

90.3 How a feed learns to make people angry

The feed is the stream of posts a site chooses to show each user. Sites, the note says, use an AI system that learns to keep users there, measured in time and clicks and called engagement. What works is things which are not true, which spread misinformation, and things which make people angry, which polarise society. Clickbait is the simplest case: the most effective link lies about where it goes, “a classic deceptive pattern”.

The Macedonian sites are the worked example:

The Google add machine was training them what to say to be rewarded with money. The Dysfunction of Social Networks, 2024

His own feed

Berners-Lee’s Instagram once showed posts by the friends and family he followed; years later it gave him “a feed of amazing videos of amazing things and products -- all very clickable and doom-scrollable”. He asks:

Is the reward for a platform that grabs my attention so great that all kinds of current social media platforms will drift toward the same identical model, and lose their originality? The Dysfunction of Social Networks, 2024

Mental health

The note admits that effects on mental health are hard to measure. In extreme cases a suicide traces back to internet use; the US Surgeon General’s suggestion of health warnings, as on cigarettes, raised public discussion. A quieter harm runs through many platforms: comparing oneself with friends, film stars or influencers. That peer pressure, the note says, “can be unhealthy and is close to fear of missing out”. Yet the Web can also help, through resources and supportive friendships.

90.4 The assumption underneath

One belief makes targeting seem necessary:

There is a widespread but questionable assumption that the only way to get revenue on the web is with targeted advertising. The Dysfunction of Social Networks, 2024

If it holds, targeting is forced; if not, it is a choice. In 90.5 he asks companies to optimise for other things, “which would not completely destroy your revenue”.

90.5 Who can fix it

For tech companies the first item is what the feed’s AI is trained to optimise: “This is a really important choice!” The common understanding, he notes, is that training for engagement ends up correlating with anger and hate; he suggests constructive discussion, exploring other cultures, or a Stretch Friend (Chapter 89). Platforms can change: the Web Foundation reported three changes women asked for on online gender-based violence, and each platform made its change. One let users opt out of Strava’s global heat map.

The rest is briefer: transparent algorithms and no deceptive patterns; legislation and “pre-bunking”, preparing people for fake news before an election; parents talking to children and teaching scepticism; protest; using the good stuff. To developers who can build better systems than the ones we have, he says:

One could argue that you have an obligation to do so. The Dysfunction of Social Networks, 2024

The note ends on method:

You can't build something as big as the web and -- or as big as a social network -- and just launch it off hoping that good things will happen. The Dysfunction of Social Networks, 2024
You have to carefully watch and research what actually happens, what unexpected consequences emerge in the behaviors of huge groups of humanity connected by technology. Then you need to re-engineer the system, technical, social, regulatory and legal, to fix the problems, and enhance the benefits. The Dysfunction of Social Networks, 2024

What happened since

  • Regulation arrived in force. The EU’s Digital Services Act became fully applicable in February 2024; the UK’s Online Safety Bill had become an Act in October 2023.
  • The evidence on children stayed contested. The Surgeon General’s call (June 2024) did not settle the causal case.
  • Third-party cookies survived in Chrome. Safari and Firefox block or isolate them by default; Google dropped its plan to remove them from Chrome in July 2024, confirming this in April 2025. Fingerprinting works either way.
  • The revenue assumption was tested. Paid tiers grew, such as Meta’s ad-free subscription in Europe (November 2023), without displacing advertising.

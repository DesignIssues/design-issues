Chapter 92

Beneficent Apps

2023

Source Beneficent Apps, a Design Issues note by Tim Berners-Lee, 2023-03-23. read the original

The short version

A program can work for the person using it, or for someone else: an advertiser, a seller, the company that makes it. People raised on web tools may assume the second is normal.

This note calls the first kind a beneficent app. The test is to ask, at every design choice, what the user would want; an app paid by selling its users’ data is unlikely to pass. Doctors and lawyers already owe such a duty, and software, especially AI, should owe it too.

The question

Berners-Lee had bought Quicken long before to do his finances and taxes. One day, having done his taxes, it asked in effect whether he was sure he had enough insurance, and whether he would like to buy some.

That was the end of an era: the era in which I trusted Quicken to be my representative and work on my behalf. Beneficent Apps, 2018

The note, begun in 2018 and revisited in 2023 because Solid (Chapter 54) needs the concept, opens: “It is a sign of the times (2010’s) that we even have to talk about these.” A beneficent app works in the interest of the person using it.

The word comes from MIT’s rules for experiments with human subjects: the work must be in the subjects’ interest, trying to cure disease rather than generate it. The idea is not foreign to the Web: the HTTP specification calls the browser a user agent.

92.1 When the program worked for its user

On the floppy-disk PCs people bought a program to do something useful or fun: play a game, fly a plane, write an essay, do their taxes. The program sat in the A: drive and the user’s data on a disk in the B: drive. That data could go to other programs, and the program was the user’s tool. It worked for them.

Quicken ended that era for him. People who have only known web-based tools, he observes of millennials, may take tools that work in someone else’s interest as the default. Something important is lost: a program that represents its user is a form of empowerment.

92.2 What a browser owes

The browser is his main example of a beneficent app.

DutyExample of failure (ours)
Help the user know which party they are talking toA look-alike bank site passes as the real one
Help the user remember where they have goneA page that cannot be found again
Help the user curate a valuable subset of the WebEverything worth keeping is lost in the flood
Store passwords and keys safelyA password saved where other sites can read it
Help the user avoid being tricked, fooled or manipulatedA trick button that signs the user up

Beneficent apps are not the norm on the Web or on mobile devices, yet they are common. Every open source application is, or should be, one, because it is built by a community for its own use, with the end user’s needs in mind.

92.3 One question, and one dealbreaker

The advice is a single question to ask at every choice:

When designing a Beneficent App, always just think at each design choice -- what would the user want the app to do? Beneficent Apps, 2018

The designer must also think of users who differ from them, in technical ability, sociability, situation and personality. And the business model can settle the matter before design begins:

If you have an income stream from selling the data from your users, then you are not likely to build a Beneficent App by default. Beneficent Apps, 2018

92.4 Usefulness is hard to count

The obstacle is measurement:

It is so easy to make metrics for non-beneficent apps: the engagement level, click-through, the ad revenue or sales revenue they provide. It is more difficult to measure how useful you have been to your user. Beneficent Apps, 2018

The note’s example is an app for organising parties. Counting parties is too rare a signal, “not to mention whether they were great parties”. So the designer measures the build-up: the amount of chat, whether it is happy and constructive, and whether tasks get done by people other than those who raised them, then checks which of these went with past parties that worked. Some usefulness never shows at all: “A user may just be really well informed of something really important, but not do anything which your app could pick up.”

Users can be surveyed, and candidate measures tried in A/B testing, where two versions go to different users and the results are compared. The note also warns of effects nobody planned:

Did they people building social networks imagine the effects on teenage health of a beauty based economy? Probably not, but now we know that systems build to be happy centers of collaboration can end up being toxic for classes of users. To be beneficent, you have to also do no harm! Beneficent Apps, 2018

92.5 Doctors and lawyers already do this

The concept of apps which work for you, the concept of something which is your agent, is not in fact foreign to our current world. We have it, after all with doctors, and with lawyers. Beneficent Apps, 2018

A doctor takes the Hippocratic Oath; a lawyer is bound to put the client first. Berners-Lee offers those bodies of law as a source for anyone drafting such commitments for software. As AI grows more powerful, he adds, that matters more at every step.

The test is a question put to the machine itself:

Hey online Ad system, Who do you work for? When you recommend I eat at a restaurant, is that the best one for, me or a the result of an instant online auction for who ever bids most to you for my custom? Hey, Siri, who do you work for? Hey, Alexa, who to you work for? Beneficent Apps, 2018

The note ends: “Can you even imagine an AI that works for you? I can, and he’s called Charlie.” Charlie is Chapter 93; the attempt to build him is Chapter 94.

What happened since

  • Regulation approached the duty. The EU Digital Services Act, applying in full from 17 February 2024, bans dark patterns in online platform interfaces in Article 25, encoding part of what section 92.2 asks designers to ask themselves.
  • Professional duties were proposed for software. Jack Balkin’s 2016 argument that data holders should be information fiduciaries applies the doctor and lawyer precedent to them. The US Data Care Act, a 2018 bill built on it, did not pass.

Connections

The agent’s duty to represent the user is Chapter 84. The separation of application from storage is Chapter 54, and the harms of the alternative are Chapter 90. The assistant this anticipates is Chapters 93 and 94, and inference over one’s own data is Chapter 72.

Questions 4

92.1 Put the note’s question, “who do you work for?”, to every application on one phone. How many have a clear answer?

92.2 For the party-organising app, the most valuable outcome may be invisible. Propose a measure of its usefulness, and say what it would miss.

92.3 He says “not likely … by default”. Describe a data-selling app that could still serve its user, or argue none can.

92.4 A lawyer is bound to put the client first. Write that rule for an assistant that recommends restaurants, so that a reader could tell whether it had been broken.

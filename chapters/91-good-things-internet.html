Chapter 91

The Good Things on the Internet

2024

Source The Good Things on the Internet, a Design Issues note by Tim Berners-Lee, 2024. read the original

The short version

Most of the internet is benign, and much of it is good. The serious harm comes mainly from feeds ranked for engagement, which a few platforms run. The good parts tend to run on open protocols, which let people choose their own app.

The question

Written in June 2024, four months after Chapter 90, and its occasion is that the previous note worked too well:

So many discussions recently have questions about how to avoid the harm that some things on the internet have done, that I had to make a map of all the good things which people should not forget. The Good on the Internet, 2024

The note is built around a large diagram of sites, apps and their effects. Figure 91.1 is a simplified sketch of it, not Berners-Lee’s map.

91.1 The proportion

The claim is quantitative, and it is the whole argument:

When you look at all of the things to do on the web, or in the apps, then the majority are actually not damaging, many are in fact good - and many are actually wonderful. The Good on the Internet, 2024

The occasion is domestic rather than political: parents worrying about harm to their children, and schools wondering whether to ban phones. A protocol, as in the green boxes of Figure 91.1, is a public set of rules that lets any app talk to any server, as email does. In the map, rounded boxes are sites and apps, diamonds are problems with the platforms, and circles are the processes they lead to in the world.

91.2 The distinction the map is drawing

The most careful passage separates two things usually treated as one. A profile is what a service learns about a person from what they do on it. It is generous about this first thing:

Platforms like, say, Amazon retail, LinkedIn, and Google search do track you and build a profile of you. That mostly helps them provide a service, connect you with people and ideas and products which you will find relevant. The Good on the Internet, 2024

Then the qualification. Third-party advertising means letting other companies buy space aimed at a person using that profile:

To the extent that they allow advertising by third parties, the platforms which profile you are capable of being part of the world in which you are manipulated by targeted advertising to do something against your own best interests The Good on the Internet, 2024

To optimise for engagement is to choose what the feed shows by what keeps people scrolling. He names Facebook, Instagram and TikTok as platforms whose feeds do this, and ranks that harm above tracking:

For the world as a whole, that can be a bigger problem than the problems which just stem from tracking, spying, profile building and targeted advertising. The Good on the Internet, 2024

The picture is not static. Instagram once showed posts from people he followed; years later it showed clickable videos and products. He asks whether the reward for grabbing attention will pull every platform toward the same model; some, he notes, fear it is inevitable.

91.3 What the map is for

The purpose is a double obligation:

We need to recognize that, make sure we and our children make the best use of it, while protecting ourselves from the harms. The Good on the Internet, 2024

The point is not that the harms are exaggerated, but that withdrawal forfeits things that help people be creative, work together, have a good time, and understand the world and the many people in it better.

91.4 Digital sovereignty

The note defines digital sovereignty broadly: people are in charge of their internet experience and can use it how they like, emailing whoever they want, bookmarking anything, and sharing only what they choose. They already have this power to a large extent, though how much depends on their expertise. Systems built on Solid, the architecture of Volume VI, naturally provide it.

91.5 The good parts run on protocols

The map covers the whole internet: besides the Web’s HTTP, much runs on other protocols.

An open protocol lets the user choose the app: the same mailbox opens in Apple Mail, Outlook or an open-source client. Web mail such as Gmail is easier, but leaves the user less power.

Once pages could carry JavaScript, the site owner chose the program the reader ran and kept the data on its own server. With no common protocol, Flickr, Facebook and LinkedIn became closed silos.

91.6 Federation and hosted chat

In a client–server system, an app talks to a server that someone runs. A federated system is one where anyone can start such a server, and the servers talk to each other; together they are called the Fediverse.

91.7 Why the volume ends here

Placed after Chapter 90, this chapter says what is at stake if the response to failure is abandonment.

What happened since

  • Warning labels. The US Surgeon General called in a New York Times opinion piece (June 2024) for warning labels on social media platforms; the note cites it.
  • Restrictions spread. Australia passed a law in November 2024 setting a minimum age of 16 for social media accounts, the kind of withdrawal this note was written to complicate.

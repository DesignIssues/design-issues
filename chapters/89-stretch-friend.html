Chapter 89

A "Stretch Friend"

2017

Source A "Stretch Friend", a Design Issues note by Tim Berners-Lee, 2017-01-01. read the original

The question

Presented in talks from 2011 onwards and written down later, this note asks whether the thing the Web is praised for is actually being used.

We celebrate the web as a space which allows you to discover all kinds of new things people and places. We admire it as something which breaks down geographical barriers, allowing me to interact with someone on the other side of the world as easily as with the person next door. But are we really using that aspect, of it? Stretch friends, 2011

89.1 What recommendation does

The mechanism is described neutrally, because there is nothing wrong with it:

The simple way xdoing this is just to pick people who are friend s of our existing friends. We are almost sure to like them, especialy if we have a lot of friends in common. Stretch friends, 2011

It works. That is the problem:

So what happens if we accept these new proposed friends? Out social network becomes a solid mass of mutually connected people. We make cliques. Stretch friends, 2011

The example he gives is the one that makes the abstraction concrete: children able to play a game with anyone logged on anywhere in the world end up playing with the child next door, or the one at the other end of the sofa. Then the question:

This is good for the cohesion of the neighborhood, but is it good for the planet? Stretch friends, 2011

89.3 The evidence he cites

The note rests on one piece of survey analysis, recounted with an explicit note to check the details. In the American election of 2008, an analyst was puzzled that the shift towards one candidate occurred almost everywhere except among one group. Those people, when asked, said they could not imagine a person of that race being president. And who were they?

Well, they were by strong correlation the people who answered "no" to the question, "Have you ever lived or worked with someone of a different race". Stretch friends, 2011

The generalisation Berners-Lee draws is about human beings rather than about that election:

So human beings have an ability to mark people outside their normal zone as being strange, or foreign, or enemy. We have evolved the neurology to become tribal at the drop of a hat Stretch friends, 2011

He cites the classroom experiment in which children were divided by eye colour and calls it rather horrid. The conclusion is stated as urgency: breaking down these single differences, which otherwise let people switch into a tribal mode, becomes a matter of urgency.

89.4 The invitation

The note ends without a design, which is unusual and appropriate. Berners-Lee notes the idea could be expanded, and connects it to town and school twinning schemes, with a parenthetical observation that when such things are arranged by churches they do not always cross religious boundaries.

Then the closing line, which is an argument about method:

Web scientists, people who analyze these things, could do lots of math to try work out what the effect would be. Or we could just try it. Stretch friends, 2011

Chapter 87 admits it has no mathematics for the shape of a healthy society. This says the shortage of mathematics is not a reason to wait, because the intervention is cheap, reversible and refusable by anyone who does not want it.

What happened since

  • Nobody built it. No major platform has offered a recommendation deliberately chosen to cross a boundary, and the incentive runs the other way: similar people engage more.
  • The diagnosis was confirmed at scale. Homophily in recommendation and the resulting narrowing of exposure became one of the most studied properties of social platforms in the following decade.
  • The contact hypothesis held up. Research since has continued to find that contact across a difference reduces prejudice, with conditions attached, which is the mechanism this proposal relies on.
  • Exposure to difference grew and did not help. Platforms increased encounters across political lines, and evidence suggests the encounters were adversarial rather than acquainted, which is a distinction the note does not draw and which turns out to matter.

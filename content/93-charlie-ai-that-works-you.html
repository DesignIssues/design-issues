The question

Written in 2017 and used in talks from 2019, this is the only piece of fiction in the collection. It is a short scene in which a man called Bob talks to an assistant called Charlie while riding an exercise bicycle, and it exists to make one architectural point vivid.

The premise is stated in the first two sentences:

Bob was fed up with the AIs around him (Alexa, Siri and so on) who all seemed to work for other people, and so he got Charlie. Charlie is an AI and Charlie works for Bob. Charlie works for Bob, 2017

93.1 The scene

What Charlie does in a few minutes is worth listing, because the range is the argument. He notes that a meeting moved and offers to extend the workout. He reports that offers came in overnight for Bob's art and that none makes sense after fees. He has invested a little in a start-up, partly because it gives Bob something in common with his mother-in-law. He has noticed that vegans are coming on Saturday.

And then the passage where the fiction does something the rest of the collection cannot:

Well he hadn't published that one, but he has published a dozen books so I read those as a training set and then extrapolated how he would cook the menu you liked. Then compared it with the Linked Open Recipe data, and adjusted it a bit for the way you like things. Charlie works for Bob, 2017

Written in 2017: learn a person's style from their published work, extrapolate to something they did not write, then reconcile it with structured public data and the user's own preferences. The first two halves of that sentence describe capabilities that arrived five years later, and the second two describe the architecture of Volume VI.

93.3 The answer that is repeated

The scene opens and closes with the same question, and the second answer is longer by three words:

Legally, ethically and algorithmically, I work for you, Bob. Charlie works for Bob, 2017

Three separate guarantees. A contract that binds. A duty of the kind Chapter 92 draws from medicine and law. And a property of how the thing actually operates, which is the only one a user could check and the hardest to provide.

93.4 How Bob got his data

The note ends by asking the question the fiction skipped, which is the one that matters:

How could this happen? How could Bob get to the point where he has access to the data? Charlie works for Bob, 2017

Three answers are offered, and none of them is technical. A backlash in which people demand access to their own data. A sector where it already happened, banking in Britain, where consumers could already use their data with other services. And regulation in Europe which, he says, basically calls for the sort of thing Charlie needs.

Volume VI built the mechanism. This says the mechanism was never the obstacle. The obstacle is that somebody else holds the data, and what changes that is law and pressure rather than protocol.

93.5 Why a story

The collection contains ninety-five arguments and one scene, and the scene does something the arguments cannot. Nobody reading Chapter 54 can feel what separating storage from applications would be like. Six exchanges over an exercise bicycle make it concrete: the assistant knows about the meeting, the art, the investment, the relatives and the recipe, because all of it is in one place that Bob controls.

The details are chosen to cross the boundaries that ordinarily separate applications. Calendar, fitness, finance, art sales, family, food. Every one is a different service today, and none of them can see the others.

What happened since

  • The capability arrived and the arrangement did not. Assistants that can extrapolate a style and reason across documents became ordinary from 2022, hosted by the companies that already hold the data.
  • The question got asked. Who an assistant works for became a mainstream concern rather than an architectural aside, which is Chapter 92's framing entering public argument.
  • Data access rights spread. Portability and open banking rules expanded through the 2020s, which are the non-technical answers section 93.4 identifies.
  • Charlie was built. Engineers attempted an implementation in late 2024, which is Chapter 94.

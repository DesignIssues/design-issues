The question

Written in 2021, this note surveys what had happened to subscription in the two decades since it was a Web technology, and asks why reacting to things requires a different account on every platform.

The definition offered is deliberately minimal:

Let's call a feed a sequence of published things such that you can subscribe to, with in some cases a mechanism to inform you when the is more added to it. So a feed by itself is a one-way thing with no feedback. Feeds, 2021

One way, no feedback. Everything interesting in the note is about what got added on top of that, and where.

64.1 The survey

64.2 Reaction as a vocabulary problem

The note looks at the vocabularies that exist for describing what people do, and finds them broad rather than narrow: an activity is an object representing some form of action that has been taken, and there are more than twenty kinds, from accepting and adding to liking, reading and undoing.

Berners-Lee then extends the idea past social media entirely:

In general, any collaborative system in which the shared state changes form time to time, like for example an issue tracker or action list, then those state-based systems cold well be deemed to create Activity Stream events whenever that state changes. Feeds, 2021

An issue tracker is a feed. So is a document being edited, a build system, a shared list. Chapter 45 made the same move from the other direction when it observed that a patch is news. Any system with changing shared state produces a stream of events whether or not anyone publishes it.

The goal that follows is about interface rather than data:

By agreeing on common interoperable representation and workflows for social actions, the Solid world can allow the same smooth user experience no matter what it it they are reacting to --liking, and so on. Feeds, 2021

64.3 The scope problem

The section that matters most is about who sees a reaction, and it identifies a structural difference that most discussion of these systems misses.

With Instagram and Strava each person has one group of followers, so a choice may be not sharing, sharing with followers, or making something completely public. Feeds, 2021

Three options, because there is one audience. A person with many overlapping groups, belonging to different communities for different reasons, needs the reaction to reach the right one, and Berners-Lee says making sure of that is very important. Chapter 74 is the treatment of that requirement in general.

The reason platforms offer three options is not that three is enough. It is that a platform that manages identity manages exactly one audience per person, and the granularity of sharing cannot exceed the granularity of the identity system underneath it.

64.4 The warning

The note does not end in advocacy. A section headed with the possibility of harm records that research and common experience both suggest these systems can produce harmful unintended consequences.

That is the two-protocol analysis of Chapter 15 applied to something being proposed rather than something being diagnosed. The technical protocol for reactions is easy. The social protocol, that people react in proportion and that counts of reactions mean what they appear to mean, is an assumption, and Chapter 90 is what happens when such an assumption turns out to be false at scale.

Designing the mechanism while recording that the mechanism may be harmful is an unusual thing for a design note to do, and it is the right thing, because the alternative is discovering it afterwards.

What happened since

  • Federated social media grew quickly. Networks built on the ActivityPub protocol, a W3C Recommendation of January 2018, expanded sharply from 2022, making the activity vocabulary this note discusses into everyday infrastructure.
  • Audience granularity stayed coarse. Federated systems mostly reproduced the three-option model of section 64.3, because they too give each person one set of followers.
  • Podcasts stayed open and reactionless. The medium with an open format and no dominant platform still has no general way to respond, which is the row in Figure 64.1 that proves the argument.
  • The harm was studied. Evidence on engagement-driven ranking and its effects accumulated through the 2020s, and Chapter 90 is Berners-Lee's own later account.

The short version

Something went wrong with the part of the Web where people talk to each other, and by 2024 its inventor thought it was worth writing down exactly what.

This chapter takes it apart mechanism by mechanism: keeping a record of what somebody did, recognising the same person across different sites, assembling a picture from the two, and then the separate and larger problem of choosing what to show in order to keep them there. The care it takes over which steps are actually harmful is what makes it worth reading.

The question

Written in February 2024 and marked final, this is the note in which Berners-Lee turns the collection’s method on the thing he built. He says why:

After writing a lot about how the Web works, it is not unreasonable to write about how it is not working. The Dysfunction of Social Networks, 2024

And narrows the subject, because everything has been written about everything:

Here we focus on arguably the deepest harm to society, the mental health of children and teens, and the rise of hatred, polarization of opinion, and threats to democracy. The Dysfunction of Social Networks, 2024

90.1 What was expected

The account of the early period is unsentimental about its own optimism:

In the early days of the web, it seemed that the world of humanity connected by technology could become a wise, warm, self-governing one. The Dysfunction of Social Networks, 2024

He cites a famous manifesto of the period and calls it a great example of a naive utopian vision, which is unusually direct about a document he would once have been associated with.

The turn is described in one sentence, and it is a structural claim rather than a complaint about content:

After a few years, people joined the social networks instead of making websites,and the social networks became monopolies. The Dysfunction of Social Networks, 2024

90.2 The chain

The care he takes over profiling is what makes the analysis credible:

Any application has to build some kind a profile of its user. Profile building is needed in order to serve the user well, if nothing else. So the profile building itself is not the problem. The problems are when that profile is used to target the user in a way as to take advantage of them The Dysfunction of Social Networks, 2024

The mechanism is not the harm. The use is.

90.3 The assumption underneath

One sentence identifies the belief that makes the whole chain seem necessary:

There is a widespread but questionable assumption that the only way to get revenue on the web is with targeted advertising. The Dysfunction of Social Networks, 2024

If that assumption holds, everything follows: revenue requires targeting, targeting requires profiles, profiles require collection and correlation. If it does not hold, none of it is forced. Berners-Lee does not argue it here; he marks it as questionable and moves on, which leaves the reader with the question.

90.4 On what “spying” covers

The note defines its own term unusually widely:

Here we use "Spying" for any retention of user data. The Dysfunction of Social Networks, 2024

And then divides it in the way that matters to a person: data the user knows about, such as what they typed and the pictures they uploaded, and data they do not, such as their address, their location, and the parameters that identify them.

The description of correlation is the most unsettling passage, because of what is on the list:

It includes things like browser type, processor speed, typing timing, and so on, which together make a characteristic signature for a person which will be consistent between different sites. The Dysfunction of Social Networks, 2024

How fast you type. Chapter 71 argued that reading should be unobserved, and this is the same act observed by a party the reader has no relationship with at all.

90.5 What the response requires

The conclusion refuses to name a single culprit or a single remedy:

The response to these challenges must be multifaceted, involving efforts from tech companies, governments, parents, and activists to mitigate the negative impacts of social networks. The Dysfunction of Social Networks, 2024

Three elements are called crucial: transparency, ethical design, and regulatory measures. And the framing of the whole, which keeps the note from being a recantation:

We need a collective effort to harness the positive potential of the internet while addressing its darker aspects. The Dysfunction of Social Networks, 2024

Chapter 91 is the other half of that sentence, written four months later, because Berners-Lee found that saying this had left people with the impression that there was nothing good left.

What happened since

  • Regulation arrived in force. The European Union’s Digital Services Act became fully applicable in February 2024, requiring risk assessment and transparency from large platforms, which is one of the three crucial elements named here.
  • The evidence on children remained contested. The claim about adolescent mental health was argued vigorously through 2024 and 2025, with researchers disputing the strength of the causal case while agreeing on the association.
  • Fingerprinting outlived cookies. Restrictions on third-party cookies pushed correlation towards exactly the signature-based methods described in section 90.4.
  • The revenue assumption began to weaken. Subscription and paid tiers grew on several major platforms, which tests the assumption section 90.3 marks as questionable, without displacing advertising.

Questions

90.1 Profiling is necessary and targeting is the harm. Draw the line precisely for a service you use.

90.2 Test the revenue assumption. Name three services funded another way and say why the model does not generalise.

90.3 Engagement optimisation is judged a larger harm than targeting. Argue for or against, using a mechanism rather than an example.

90.4 Typing rhythm identifies a person across sites. What would it take to stop that, and who would have to act?

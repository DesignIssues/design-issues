The short version

Social networks earn money by collecting data about each user so that advertisers can target them. Separately, their feeds learn to show whatever keeps people scrolling, and that is often false or enraging.

This 2024 note separates the harmful steps from the harmless ones, and ends with a list of who can fix them.

The question

Written in February 2024, updated later that year and marked final, this is the note in which Berners-Lee turns the collection’s method on the thing he built. He says why:

After writing a lot about how the Web works, it is not unreasonable to write about how it is not working. The Dysfunction of Social Networks, 2024

And narrows the subject, because everything has been written about everything:

Here we focus on arguably the deepest harm to society, the mental health of children and teens, and the rise of hatred, polarization of opinion, and threats to democracy. The Dysfunction of Social Networks, 2024

90.1 What was expected

Of the early period:

In the early days of the web, it seemed that the world of humanity connected by technology could become a wise, warm, self-governing one. The Dysfunction of Social Networks, 2024

He calls John Perry Barlow’s A Declaration of the Independence of Cyberspace (1996) a great example of a naive utopian vision. Yet the early years had a lot that was good: anyone could make their own website and link it to other people’s. Then came the turn:

After a few years, people joined the social networks instead of making websites,and the social networks became monopolies. The Dysfunction of Social Networks, 2024

90.2 The chain

A profile is the picture a service keeps of a user: interests, habits, contacts. Targeting is choosing which advertisement each person sees according to it. The note sets out the steps as a chain.

He is careful about profiling:

Any application has to build some kind a profile of its user. Profile building is needed in order to serve the user well, if nothing else. So the profile building itself is not the problem. The problems are when that profile is used to target the user in a way as to take advantage of them The Dysfunction of Social Networks, 2024

How a feed learns to make people angry

The feed is the stream of posts a site chooses to show each user. The note says sites use an AI system that learns whatever keeps a user there; the time spent and clicks it counts are called engagement.

What works, it reports, is two kinds of item:

  • things which are not true, which spread misinformation and damage a democracy that needs informed voters;
  • things which make people angry, which make the online world toxic and polarise society into hostile camps.

Clickbait is the simplest case. If the goal is a click, the most effective link lies about where it goes, and the note calls this “a classic deceptive pattern”.

The worked example is from the 2016 US election. Young people in Macedonia made a living from websites funded by Google advertisements, trying headlines and keeping whichever drew most traffic:

The Google add machine was training them what to say to be rewarded with money. The Dysfunction of Social Networks, 2024

So untruths were spread by people with “no skin in the game”. The incentive did the choosing.

Mental health

The note admits that the effects on mental health would be hard to catalogue and measure. In extreme cases a suicide can be traced to a person’s use of the internet, and the US Surgeon General’s suggestion of cigarette-style health warnings raised public discussion. Comparing oneself with friends and influencers adds unhealthy peer pressure. But the note adds that the Web can also improve mental health, through access to resources and supportive friendships.

90.3 The assumption underneath

One belief makes targeting seem necessary:

There is a widespread but questionable assumption that the only way to get revenue on the web is with targeted advertising. The Dysfunction of Social Networks, 2024

If it holds, targeting is forced. If not, targeting is a choice. Berners-Lee marks it questionable and leaves the question open.

90.4 On what “spying” covers

The note defines the term widely:

Here we use "Spying" for any retention of user data. The Dysfunction of Social Networks, 2024

Some of that data the user knows about, such as forms and photos. Some they do not: their IP address (the numeric address of their internet connection), their location, and super-cookie parameters (hidden identifiers that survive clearing cookies).

Correlation means concluding that two records describe the same person. The note explains how:

It includes things like browser type, processor speed, typing timing, and so on, which together make a characteristic signature for a person which will be consistent between different sites. The Dysfunction of Social Networks, 2024

Such a signature is called a fingerprint. Some browsers, such as Tor Browser, resist it by making every user look alike. Chapter 71 argued that what a person reads should not be watched. Fingerprinting lets sites the reader has no relationship with watch it anyway.

90.5 What the response requires

The conclusion spreads the work:

The response to these challenges must be multifaceted, involving efforts from tech companies, governments, parents, and activists to mitigate the negative impacts of social networks. The Dysfunction of Social Networks, 2024
WhoWhat the note suggests
Tech companiesOptimise the feed for something other than time on site, such as constructive discussion or a Stretch Friend (Chapter 89); be transparent about algorithms; don’t use deceptive patterns. The Web Foundation reported three changes women asked for on gender-based abuse, and each platform made its change. One let users opt out of Strava’s global heat map.
GovernmentsLegislation; and “pre-bunking”, preparing people for fake news before an election.
ParentsTalk to children, use parental controls, teach scepticism.
ActivistsProtest.
EveryoneUse the good stuff.
DevelopersBuild better stuff.

Its last sentence applies the collection’s method to society:

Then you need to re-engineer the system, technical, social, regulatory and legal, to fix the problems, and enhance the benefits. The Dysfunction of Social Networks, 2024

What happened since

  • Regulation arrived in force. The European Union’s Digital Services Act became fully applicable in February 2024. The UK measure the note calls a Bill had become the Online Safety Act in October 2023.
  • The evidence on children stayed contested. In June 2024 the US Surgeon General called for a warning label on social media, as the note records; researchers still dispute the strength of the causal case.
  • Third-party cookies survived in Chrome. Safari (March 2020) and Firefox (June 2022) block or isolate third-party cookies by default; Google dropped its plan to remove them from Chrome in July 2024, confirming this in April 2025. Fingerprinting works either way.
  • The revenue assumption was tested. Paid tiers grew, such as Meta’s ad-free subscription in Europe (November 2023), without displacing advertising.

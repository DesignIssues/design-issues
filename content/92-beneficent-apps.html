The question

Volume X opens with a note begun in 2018 and revisited in 2023, and Berners-Lee explains where the word comes from. It is from the rules governing experiments with human subjects at his own institution: besides being informed, the subjects must be the beneficiaries. The work must be trying to cure disease rather than generate it.

He also notes that the idea is not foreign to the Web, since the specification already calls a browser a user agent.

92.1 The moment

The argument is made through one incident, recounted precisely.

Until, that is, for me one day when Quicken, the program I had bought ages ago to do my finances and taxes, when it had done my taxes, asked words to the effect of "Are you sure you have enough insurance? Would you like to buy some great insurance?" Beneficent Apps, 2018

And the verdict on it:

That was the end of an era: the era in which I trusted Quicken to be my representative and work on my behalf. Beneficent Apps, 2018

The list of duties is specific enough to audit against, and every item is something a browser can fail at silently.

Berners-Lee adds that beneficent applications are not the norm on the Web or on mobile devices and are nonetheless common, because every open source application should be one: developed by a community for its own use, with the needs of the end user in mind.

92.3 The design question, and the disqualifying condition

The advice is a single question to ask at every choice:

When designing a Beneficent App, always just think at each design choice -- what would the user want the app to do? Beneficent Apps, 2018

With the qualification that the designer must think of users who differ from them, in technical ability, in sociability, in situation and in personality.

And then the sentence that decides the matter before any design begins:

If you have an income stream from selling the data from your users, then you are not likely to build a Beneficent App by default. Beneficent Apps, 2018

92.4 The measurement problem

The most useful section is about why the good version is harder to run as a business, and it is not about ethics:

It is so easy to make metrics for non-beneficent apps: the engagement level, click-through, the ad revenue or sales revenue they provide. It is more difficult to measure how useful you have been to your user. Beneficent Apps, 2018

The difficulty is named exactly: a user may be really well informed of something really important and do nothing the application can observe. The most valuable outcome is invisible.

The worked example is an application for organising parties, and Berners-Lee follows it properly. Count the parties organised. But those come infrequently, so measure the things leading to them: the amount of chat, whether it is happy and constructive, whether tasks in a shared list get done by people other than those who raised them. Then check which of those actually accompanied good parties, and decide which to optimise for.

The warning attached is the note's conclusion, and it names a specific harm:

Did they people building social networks imagine the effects on teenage health of a beauty based economy? Probably not, but now we know that systems build to be happy centers of collaboration can end up being toxic for classes of users. To be beneficent, you have to also do no harm! Beneficent Apps, 2018

92.5 The precedent

The closing argument is that agents bound to a client's interest already exist:

The concept of apps which work for you, the concept of something which is your agent, is not in fact foreign to our current world. We have it, after all with doctors, and with lawyers. Beneficent Apps, 2018

A doctor takes an oath; a lawyer is bound to put the client first. Berners-Lee suggests those bodies of law as a source to draw on for anyone wanting to write such commitments for software.

The last section applies it to what was coming, and the test he proposes is four words long:

Hey, Siri, who do you work for? Hey, Alexa, who to you work for? Beneficent Apps, 2018

What happened since

  • The question became the standard framing. Asking whose interest an assistant serves is now central to debate about these systems, and the answer for the major assistants is still their maker's.
  • Regulation approached the duty. Rules on dark patterns and on design that exploits users, in European platform law from 2024, encode part of what section 92.3 asks designers to ask themselves.
  • The measurement problem persists. Nobody has a standard way to measure usefulness to a user, so products are still steered by engagement, which is the mechanism of Chapter 90.
  • Professional duties were proposed for software. Information fiduciary arguments, which apply the doctor and lawyer precedent to data holders, gained ground in legal scholarship without becoming law.

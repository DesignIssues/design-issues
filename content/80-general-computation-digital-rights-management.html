The question

Written in 2013 during one of the most acrimonious arguments in the Consortium’s history, and revised in 2023 to look back at how it turned out. Berners-Lee describes the state of the debate before entering it:

In the fervent discussion, very extreme positions have been taken, which has led to the debate becoming acrimonious, to the extent that much more heat than light tends to be available. Here we tease out some separate issues which have become entangled. General Computation, Digital Rights Management, and FOSS, 2013

The method is the contribution. Separating the entangled claims does not resolve them, and it does show why no resolution is available.

80.2 The tactical argument

The case for allowing a hook in the standard is not that the mechanism is good. It is an argument about where people end up otherwise:

there are companies who will never put their movies on the net without DRM, so basically if we don't put DRM hooks in HTML they will just stick with flash and force you install an app, or a completely closed platform like a set-top box - in other words use a completely locked down platform. General Computation, Digital Rights Management, and FOSS, 2013

And the conclusion drawn from it:

So it isn't as though making DRM more difficult in HTML5 will make DRM go away: it will just force users off the web. General Computation, Digital Rights Management, and FOSS, 2013

Berners-Lee then states the choice in its bluntest form, which is the form the argument actually took: refuse to collaborate and drive people to applications where the whole thing is locked down, or open a slot in an open system through which a locked-down system may be reached.

80.3 Four positions, fairly stated

The note’s most unusual passage sets out what different people believe, without labelling any of them wrong.

PositionWhat it rests on
Copyright itself is wrongArtists should be supported by performance and donation
I judge when to copyThe law has been twisted, and protection blocks fair use, backups, and archives that later stop working
Protection is worth it for a thriving industryNot troubled by the archive problem, and without an answer for artists outside the channels
I object to hardware I do not controlIndependent of any wish to copy anything

The second position contains the prediction that has aged best: that protected files typically fail in the future when the supporting system changes, and an archive becomes unusable. The fourth is the one that separates the argument from copyright entirely.

Berners-Lee closes the section with the question he says is related and that nobody was asking:

A related question is, what sort of systems can we build to help people give money to those who e.g. write or perform music, with or without DRM, in an open market, with no 3rd party gatekeeper? General Computation, Digital Rights Management, and FOSS, 2013

80.4 A decade on

The 2023 revision is a rare thing: an author auditing his own decision. He records what was standardised and what it does, in plain terms:

This allows the web site to get access to one of a small set of DRM gadget on the device -- gadgets which the user has no control of. General Computation, Digital Rights Management, and FOSS, 2023

Then the assessment. Streaming happened at enormous scale, on the Web and off. The competition between sites and applications continued, and the platforms still push people towards applications for more control of the environment. On his own position, he reports a check:

As a developer, I can still develop code on my Apple laptop and still install open source code and random apps written by other people on it. General Computation, Digital Rights Management, and FOSS, 2023

The right to control the general-purpose computer survived, which was the thing the objectors said was at risk. And then the finding that goes the other way:

As an artist, there is no way for me to make my own DRM platform if a person wants to protect their material. So they have to find a route to the big platforms. General Computation, Digital Rights Management, and FOSS, 2023

The mechanism was standardised, and only a handful of parties can implement it. An artist who wants protection must go through them, and Berners-Lee notes without elaboration that those platforms are infamous for returning very little to the original artists. The question at the end of section 80.3 is still unanswered.

What happened since

  • The specification was published. Encrypted Media Extensions became a W3C Recommendation on 18 September 2017, over formal objection, and the Electronic Frontier Foundation resigned from the Consortium in protest.
  • The archive prediction came true. Purchased media has repeatedly become unplayable when a protection service was retired, which is the second position’s specific warning.
  • Implementation stayed concentrated. A small number of protection systems, all controlled by large firms, are what browsers support, so the barrier section 80.4 describes remains.
  • The payment question found partial answers. Direct patronage and subscription platforms grew substantially, supplying some of what the closing question asks for, without solving it for protected works.

Connections

The open platform sense of openness is Chapter 25, and general computation is its subject. The laws that colour this debate are named there too. The argument about where people end up if the Web refuses them is Chapter 65, and the concentration of implementers is Chapter 78.

The short version

In this final chapter, Berners-Lee, trying to describe a new arrangement of the Web, remembers trying to describe the first one, when there were only a handful of websites and he was persuading somebody to make the sixth.

He could not imagine then what would be built. He says so, and then argues that this is a reason to build openly rather than a reason to wait.

The question

Written in December 2024, the note’s subject is a difficulty the author has met before:

There has been a common problem in all this, though, that it is really hard for people to imagine what this new world will be like. The Vision of a new World, 2024

96.1 The admission

It was really hard to explain to people what it would be like once the web had taken off, when there are only a few websites and I was persuading them to make the sixth. I could say "imagine a world with all the online documents linked together and instantly available on the net", but even then I could not imagine Google search, Wikipedia, Github, and XKCD. The Vision of a new World, 2024

Then the conclusion he draws about what he was doing:

So I had to persuade a person to get involved with the web, even though neither of us could really imagine how exciting and powerful it would become. The Vision of a new World, 2024

96.2 What cannot be imagined this time

The new arrangement is Solid: each person, or community, keeps data in a pod, a personal store on the Web that any application they permit can read and write. What is hard to picture is a store that is not tied to one kind of thing.

On deployment the note is frank: the software is stable and exists in several compatible versions, some open and some commercial, and almost nobody is offering it to the public. The Flanders government provides pods to its citizens, but for a limited number of purposes.

96.3 The value of connection

The economic argument borrows a law about networks. Bob Metcalfe, inventor of Ethernet, proposed that a network’s value grows with the square of its members: a telephone is worth more the more people it can call. The note applies this to applications sharing a pod:

In the vision of the world where everyone does everything with Solid apps on Solid pods, the extra value is not just from the number of apps a person uses. The apps all connect with each other, in a network of apps -- and so the extra value from those connections goes as the square. Hence the difficulty in people seeing the point of getting Solid for just one app. The Vision of a new World, 2024

People advise picking a killer app and rolling it out; the note answers that everyone’s killer app is different.

A glimpse exists inside one large platform. An email in Gmail asks for a meeting; a couple of clicks add it to Google Calendar, create a Google Meet link and send the link to everyone invited. Solid would offer that kind of integration across every application a person uses, not only within one company’s.

Linking across applications

The note’s slogan for this is “you can do anything with anything”. Today an event lists its invitees from the user’s contacts, but from a contact there is no way to find the events they were invited to. The note’s chain of steps shows what changes when the links run both ways.

The note adds a colleague’s travel example: a traveller tells an airline’s app which flight they want, so it knows where they will be and when, yet it never tells Google Maps where they need to be at the airport.

What changes for users

First, pains disappear. A photo album on Flickr could be shared with friends on Facebook and colleagues on LinkedIn at once, because the platforms have become Solid-compatible, whether under pressure or by law, or because users have moved to open ones. Medical records could move into a person’s own store, to be analysed by apps and AI and shared with chosen doctors and family; at least four groups were writing code to bring medical data into pods.

Second, new things appear. “No code” apps, driven by a data file and assembled by dragging components, are easy for users to copy and tweak.

Who pays

The note answers with familiar models: free at first with heavier users paying, as with consumer cloud storage; bundled with an email account, a domain name or a club membership; or run by a co-operative whose users share the costs. Its opening summary puts economics second:

The article underscores the economic potential of Solid-driven innovations, not as an end goal but as a byproduct of enhanced productivity and creativity, collaboration and compassion. The Vision of a new World, 2024

96.4 Communities of every size

The closing vision names the unit it cares about, and it is not the individual:

It envisions a future where empowered individuals and communities of all sizes work together to tackle global challenges, marking a paradigm shift in how we use technology to shape the world. The Vision of a new World, 2024

The body of the note explains why. The Web was always meant for collaboration: one person may have a problem in mind and another, across the planet, the solution. What carries the solution across is overlapping communities, and he lists his own, from family and teams to W3C groups and companies. Today, passing something from one to another means copying a message between WhatsApp, Slack and email. ActivityPub applications are starting to make this smooth for chat; Solid, he says, could do it for every type of data. This is Chapter 87’s requirement, stated as an outcome rather than a design constraint.

96.5 How the book ends

Ninety-six chapters, written from 1996 to 2025, end with an admission that the author could not imagine what his own work would produce, and an argument that this is a reason to proceed rather than to wait. Three early pieces of advice are all about acting without knowing what comes next:

ChapterAdviceWhat it assumes cannot be known
1Choose the least powerful means; assume someone else will independently invent the same thingWhat others will do with the work, or build beside it
16Design so that version four can read version fiveWhat the next version will contain
19Build interfaces for systems that have not been imaginedWhat will be connected later

Many of the notes record decisions taken by someone who said he could not see far, and who chose each time to keep things open so that others could improve them. Whether that approach will work a second time is not yet known.

What happened since

  • The Flemish pods have an operator. Athumi, the data utility company set up by the Flemish government and launched in 2023, runs the citizen pods the note mentions.
  • Portability rights strengthened. The EU’s Digital Markets Act obligations on the largest platforms, including data portability, applied from March 2024, and the Data Act, which gives users access to data from their connected devices, applied from September 2025. This is the non-technical route Chapter 93 identified.

The question

This is the only comic item in the collection. It was written for and performed at the Advisory Committee dinner in May 2000, while an argument raged over whether an XML namespace should be considered a web resource. Berners-Lee wrote a Socratic dialogue; Ian Jacobs played Achilles.

It is kept at full chapter length because the joke is an argument, and the argument is one the collection makes nowhere else so economically.

29.1 The setup

The frame is borrowed and credited. Berners-Lee invokes Douglas Hofstadter’s Gödel, Escher, Bach, its dialogues between Achilles and the Tortoise, and the moral he takes from several hundred pages on fugues, recursive drawings and incompleteness:

you can't try to distinuish wishes from metawishes, or the whole system breaks down. Dictionaries in the Library?, 2000

The substitution is exact. Library is to Web as dictionary is to namespace, and the debate was whether the second kind of thing may live in the first.

29.2 The Tortoise’s objections

The comedy is in the escalation, and each rung is a real position someone held.

The Tortoise saysThe move being made
It was not a goal to register dictionaries in the libraryAbsence of a goal treated as a prohibition
A dictionary is not a book, it is a metabookA new category invented to exclude the case
You wanted it in the form of a book; that was not the intentForm conceded, membership denied
Things get lost, libraries burn downRisk offered as an argument about category
We have plans for a vault, and a white towerAn alternative home, not yet built
Most of us keep a pocket dictionary anywayPresent practice as a reason not to generalise
If you don't take it out, I will bite your heelThe argument concludes

Achilles’ replies are all one reply, given three ways. He needs to look words up while working in the library, so the dictionary must be in the library. He is working in the section on library architecture, and needs a dictionary of that field’s terms. And finally the argument from the design itself:

But I thought when we designed the library it was so that any sort of book could go in it. That is why we called it the Global Eternal Bibliotech, after all: it is Good for Every Book. Dictionaries in the Library?, 2000

The joke in the acronym is the argument in Chapter 2. If the space is universal, a thing cannot be excluded from it for being the wrong kind of thing, because universality is precisely the claim that there is no wrong kind.

29.3 The line the Tortoise will not cross

One exchange is sharper than the rest, and it is about meaning:

T: Let's not discuss meaning, please Achilles -- you know what happens when we do that! I want to use these dictionaries in order to check that the books are correct. Dictionaries in the Library?, 2000

The Tortoise wants validation and refuses semantics. That division, between those who wanted schemas for checking structure and those who wanted them for conveying meaning, is the same split Chapter 32 records at the query language workshop. Berners-Lee gives the Tortoise the better joke, a reference to “current linguistic epistemorthosemantisophologic theory”, and gives Achilles the better position.

Chapter 22, written twelve years later, is the serious version of Achilles’ answer: one need not settle what meaning is in order to look something up.

What happened since

  • Achilles won, eventually and partially. The Technical Architecture Group’s resolution of namespaceDocument-8, in 2005, held that a namespace URI should dereference to a document describing the namespace. Dictionaries are in the library.
  • The shelves are mostly empty. As Chapter 9 records, many namespace URIs still return nothing useful, so the right to shelve a dictionary was won and not much exercised.
  • The same argument recurs. Whether a schema, a registry or an index belongs in the space it describes, or in a separate space with different rules, is still asked of every new system, and the answer is still usually Achilles’.
  • The performance was not recorded. The text survives; the dinner does not.

Questions

29.1 The Tortoise invents the category “metabook” to exclude dictionaries. Name a category invented in a system you know to exclude a case the design did not anticipate.

29.2 Achilles argues from the name of the library. Reconstruct that argument formally, and say which axiom of Chapter 2 it appeals to.

29.3 The Tortoise wants dictionaries for validation, not meaning. Give a case where the two purposes genuinely require different mechanisms.

29.4 Hofstadter’s moral is that separating wishes from metawishes breaks the system. State the corresponding claim about resources and metaresources, and say whether it holds.

The short version

An XML namespace is a named vocabulary: the set of tag names a kind of document uses. It is named by a URI that looks like a web address, such as http://www.w3.org/1999/xhtml for XHTML. In 2000 some held that this URI was only a label, with nothing to fetch from it. Berners-Lee held that anything with a URI may be on the Web, including the definition of a vocabulary. This chapter is his joke about that dispute.

The question

This is the only comic item in the collection. It was written for and performed at the Advisory Committee dinner in May 2000, while an argument raged over whether an XML namespace should be considered a web resource. Berners-Lee wrote a Socratic dialogue, an argument carried on as conversation; Ian Jacobs played Achilles.

29.1 The setup

The frame is borrowed and credited. Berners-Lee invokes Douglas Hofstadter’s Gödel, Escher, Bach, whose dialogues are spoken by Achilles and the Tortoise, the runners of Zeno’s paradox. He takes the moral of its several hundred pages on fugues, recursive drawings and incompleteness:

you can't try to distinuish wishes from metawishes, or the whole system breaks down. Dictionaries in the Library?, 2000

A metawish is a wish about wishes, such as wishing for more wishes. Read “library” as “the Web” and “dictionary” as “namespace”, and the dialogue is the debate: may the defining kind of thing live among the things it defines?

29.2 The Tortoise’s objections

The comedy is in the escalation, and each rung was a real position.

The Tortoise saysThe move being made
It was not a goal to register dictionaries in the libraryAbsence of a goal treated as a prohibition
A dictionary is not a book, it is a metabookA new category invented to exclude the case
Achilles wanted it in the form of a book; that was not the intentForm conceded, membership denied
Things get lost, libraries burn downRisk offered as an argument about category
We have plans for a vault, and a white towerAn alternative home, not yet built
Most of us keep a pocket dictionary anywayPresent practice as a reason not to generalise
If you don't take it out, I will bite your heelThe argument concludes

Several rungs have plain counterparts. The first echoes the 1999 Namespaces in XML Recommendation, which said it was not a goal that a namespace name be usable to retrieve a schema. The vault down the road is a separate registry for vocabularies, rather than ordinary web addresses. The pocket dictionary is software that has the vocabularies it knows built in.

Achilles gives one reply three ways: he needs to look words up in the library; his own section, on library architecture, needs a dictionary of its terms; and, finally, the design itself:

But I thought when we designed the library it was so that any sort of book could go in it. That is why we called it the Global Eternal Bibliotech, after all: it is Good for Every Book. Dictionaries in the Library?, 2000

The initials spell GEB, the usual short name for Hofstadter’s book. The acronym is also the argument of Chapter 2.

29.3 The line the Tortoise will not cross

One exchange is sharper than the rest, and it is about meaning:

T: Let's not discuss meaning, please Achilles -- you know what happens when we do that! I want to use these dictionaries in order to check that the books are correct. Dictionaries in the Library?, 2000

The Tortoise wants validation and refuses semantics. A schema is a formal description of what a kind of document may contain, and the split between those who wanted schemas for checking structure and those who wanted them for conveying meaning is close to the one Chapter 32 records at the query language workshop. Berners-Lee gives the Tortoise the better joke, a reference to “current linguistic epistemorthosemantisophologic theory”, and gives Achilles the better position.

Chapter 22, written twelve years later, is the serious version of Achilles’ answer: one need not settle what meaning is in order to look something up.

What happened since

  • Achilles won, eventually and partially. Architecture of the World Wide Web, Volume One (W3C Recommendation, 15 December 2004) advises namespace owners to publish material for people and for software, so that dereferencing the URI (fetching what it points to) returns something. Dictionaries are in the library.
  • The shelves are mostly empty. As Chapter 20 records, many namespace URIs still return nothing useful, so the right to shelve a dictionary was won and not much exercised.
  • The same argument recurs. Whether a schema, a registry or an index belongs in the space it describes, or in a separate space with different rules, is still asked of every new system, and the answer is still usually Achilles’.

Questions

29.1 The Tortoise invents the category “metabook” to exclude dictionaries. Name a category invented in a system you know to exclude a case the design did not anticipate.

29.2 Write Achilles’ argument from the name of the library as three short steps, and say which principle from Chapter 2 the last step relies on.

29.3 The Tortoise wants dictionaries for validation, not meaning. Give a case where the two purposes genuinely require different mechanisms.

29.4 The Tortoise says dictionaries “have such different requirements to books”. Name one requirement a vocabulary definition might have that an ordinary web page does not, and say whether it justifies a separate system.

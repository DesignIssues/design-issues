The question

On 26 June 2010 a workshop opened on the future of the model. Berners-Lee could not attend, so he wrote down his position instead, and scoped it tightly:

This does not address the many other exciting things which need to be done in the semantic web, but just the RDF model and its common serializations. The Future of RDF, 2010

It is a list, and the interest lies in which items were acted on, which were not, and what the pattern of each says about how a deployed standard can and cannot change.

47.2 The one that mattered most

Buried without emphasis in the middle of the list is a change to the model itself:

In the RDF model, add the ability of the subject to take any of the forms allowed for the object. Currently, this means including literals The Future of RDF, 2010

The model permits a value to appear as the object of a statement and not as its subject, so nothing can be said about a value. That is the obstruction Chapter 38 ran into: to say that a string has an interpretation, one must first invent a node for it, which Berners-Lee there called frightful. This proposal removes the obstruction in one line, and it was not adopted.

47.3 How hard to push

One paragraph is worth more than the list, because it is about the politics of changing a standard rather than the content of the change:

How strong should the deprecation be? One possibility is for them to be just best practice notes. It will have the great advantage of simplifying the documentation. The Future of RDF, 2010

The benefit claimed for removing an unused feature is not a cleaner model but shorter documentation. That is an honest accounting: a feature nobody uses costs almost nothing at runtime and costs every new reader a page.

He also proposes a migration path rather than a cliff, suggesting that sequences be converted to lists as a parser option, for systems that handle one and not the other. That is the evolvability argument of Chapter 16 applied by the same author to his own design: give implementations a way to move rather than a date by which to have moved.

47.4 The small ergonomic complaint

One proposal is about nothing more than typing effort, and it is the most human item in the collection's technical notes. Berners-Lee wants dates and times to be writable without ceremony:

Date-times are a common and important datatype and reading and writing them with an explicit datatype is boring. The Future of RDF, 2010

And then the consequence, which is not about boredom at all:

In practice people end up putting them in data on the web as strings which prevents processing software and user agents providing appropriate functionality The Future of RDF, 2010

Friction in a notation does not merely annoy. It changes what people publish, and therefore what software can do with what they published. A date written as a string cannot be sorted, compared or displayed in a local calendar. The inconvenience of the correct form is paid by every consumer of the data forever.

47.5 Consistency as a constraint on proposals

One line sets a rule for everything else on the list:

Extensions to the RDF model should be done consistently with N3 to avoid unnecessary incompatibility. The Future of RDF, 2010

A research language and a standard exist side by side, and Berners-Lee's instruction is that when the standard grows it should grow towards the research language, so that the two do not diverge into rival vocabularies for the same thing. This is the test of independent invention applied pre-emptively, to two systems by the same author.

What happened since

  • The documentation proposals were carried out. RDF 1.1, a W3C Recommendation of February 2014, defines the model independently of any syntax and includes a JSON serialisation, both of which are on this list.
  • The deprecations were softened to advice. Reification, bags and sequences remain in the specification and are avoided by convention, which is the weakest of the options section 47.3 considers.
  • Literals as subjects came back. The proposal was revived in the RDF 1.2 work begun in 2022, which is twelve years after this note and still under way.
  • Graphs in statements arrived partially. RDF-star work from 2021 onward lets a statement be the subject of another, which is the narrower half of the proposal here.

The question

Written in June 2024, four months after Chapter 90, and its occasion is that the previous note worked too well:

So many discussions recently have questions about how to avoid the harm that some things on the internet have done, that I had to make a map of all the good things which people should not forget. The Good on the Internet, 2024

The note is a companion to a diagram, which is unusual in this collection and makes this the one chapter where the figure is the primary text.

91.1 The proportion

The claim is quantitative, and Berners-Lee states it three times in different words because it is the whole argument:

When you look at all of the things to do on the web, or in the apps, then the majority are actually not damaging, many are in fact good - and many are actually wonderful. The Good on the Internet, 2024

The occasion is named precisely, and it is domestic rather than political: parents worrying about harm to their children, and schools wondering whether to ban telephones.

91.2 The distinction the map is drawing

The most careful passage separates two things that are usually treated as one, and it is generous about the first:

Platforms like, say, Amazon retail, LinkedIn, and Google search do track you and build a profile of you. That mostly helps them provide a service, connect you with people and ideas and products which you will find relevant. The Good on the Internet, 2024

Then the qualification, stated as a capability rather than an accusation:

To the extent that they allow advertising by third parties, the platforms which profile you are capable of being part of the world in which you are manipulated by targeted advertising to do something against your own best interests The Good on the Internet, 2024

And then the judgement that ranks the two harms, repeating Chapter 90's finding:

For the world as a whole, that can be a bigger problem than the problems which just stem from tracking, spying, profile building and targeted advertising. The Good on the Internet, 2024

91.3 What the map is for

The purpose is stated as a double obligation, and the second half is what distinguishes this from reassurance:

We need to recognize that, make sure we and our children make the best use of it, while protecting ourselves from the harms. The Good on the Internet, 2024

Not that the harms are exaggerated. That responding to them by withdrawal forfeits things that help people be creative, work together, have a good time, and understand the world and the many people in it better.

91.4 Why the volume ends here

Volume IX has moved from the shape of a window to the shape of a society. It began with a complaint about two metaphors on one screen and ends with a map of what the thing became.

Placing this chapter after Chapter 90 rather than before it matters. The sequence is: here is how it fails, and here is what is at stake if the response to failure is abandonment. A book that stopped at the previous chapter would have described the harm and left the reader with nothing to protect.

The term Berners-Lee introduces for the third category on the map, digital sovereignty, is the name he gives to what Volume VI was building. A person who holds their own data is not exposed to the chain of Chapter 90, because the chain begins with somebody else holding it.

What happened since

  • The phone bans happened. Restrictions on telephones in schools spread through many countries during 2024 and 2025, which is the response this note was written to complicate.
  • The pre-web categories kept working. Mail, chat, feeds and podcasts continued to operate without engagement ranking, which is the existence proof the map relies on.
  • Federated alternatives grew. Networks without a single owner expanded substantially, offering the third category at a larger scale than when this was written.
  • The map itself did not spread. The diagram remains a personal artefact rather than a reference, though the argument that most of the Web is benign became a common corrective in public debate.

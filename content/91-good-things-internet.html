The short version

Most of the internet is benign, and much of it is good. The serious harm comes mainly from feeds ranked for engagement, which a few platforms run. The good parts tend to run on open protocols, which let people choose their own app.

The question

Parents were worrying about harm to their children, and some discussions suggested “that children should not be allowed phones at all”. In June 2024, four months after Chapter 90, Berners-Lee answered with a map:

So many discussions recently have questions about how to avoid the harm that some things on the internet have done, that I had to make a map of all the good things which people should not forget. The Good on the Internet, 2024

91.1 Most of the map is not the problem

The claim is about proportion:

When you look at all of the things to do on the web, or in the apps, then the majority are actually not damaging, many are in fact good - and many are actually wonderful. The Good on the Internet, 2024

Figure 91.1 simplifies the note’s map. Rounded boxes are sites and apps, diamonds are problems with the platforms, and circles are what they lead to in the world. Green boxes are protocols: public rules that let any app talk to any server, as email does.

91.2 Tracking is not the worst of it

The most careful passage separates two things usually treated as one. A profile is what a service learns about a person from what they do on it. The note is generous about this first thing:

Platforms like, say, Amazon retail, LinkedIn, and Google search do track you and build a profile of you. That mostly helps them provide a service, connect you with people and ideas and products which you will find relevant. The Good on the Internet, 2024

Third-party advertising means letting other companies buy space aimed at a person using that profile:

To the extent that they allow advertising by third parties, the platforms which profile you are capable of being part of the world in which you are manipulated by targeted advertising to do something against your own best interests The Good on the Internet, 2024

To optimise for engagement is to choose what the feed shows by what keeps people scrolling. He names Facebook, Instagram and TikTok as platforms whose feeds do this, and ranks that harm above tracking:

For the world as a whole, that can be a bigger problem than the problems which just stem from tracking, spying, profile building and targeted advertising. The Good on the Internet, 2024

The picture is not static. Instagram once showed him posts from people he followed. Years later it gave him “a feed of amazing videos of amazing things and products -- all very clickable and doom-scrollable - but now it’s no longer useful as a private place of private friends and family.” He asks whether every platform will drift toward the same model.

91.3 What withdrawal gives up

We need to recognize that, make sure we and our children make the best use of it, while protecting ourselves from the harms. The Good on the Internet, 2024

The harms are real, but withdrawal forfeits things that help people be creative, work together, have a good time, and understand the world better.

91.4 Digital sovereignty

The note calls the user’s power over their own internet experience digital sovereignty: emailing whoever one wants, bookmarking anything on the web, sharing aspects of one’s life with friends, family and colleagues, or not. People already have this to a large extent. The catch is that it “does depend on your level of expertise”, because “the operating system vendors are constantly tempted” to make choosing an app hard (the note cites the antitrust cases against Microsoft and Apple). Solid, the architecture of Volume VI, provides it naturally.

91.5 The good parts run on protocols

Much of the internet runs on protocols other than the Web’s HTTP.

SMTP, IMAP, POPEmail clientsEmails

CalDAV, CardDAVCalendar, contacts appsEvents, contacts and groups

An open protocol lets the user choose the app: the same mailbox opens in Apple Mail, Outlook or an open-source client. Web mail such as Gmail is easier, but leaves the user less power.

Then browsers let a page carry JavaScript, a program that runs inside the page. “Much more fun. But also a power shift.” Instead of the user picking a favourite app, the site owner sends whatever program it likes and keeps the data on its own server. With no common protocol, Flickr, Facebook and LinkedIn became closed silos.

As the note has it, Jack Dorsey, making Twitter, asked himself, “Should I make a new web platform, or should I make a new protocol?” He built twitter.com, later called that a mistake, and meanwhile had to design special back-end software “to do the right thing when Justin Bieber (say) tweeted.”

91.6 Federation and hosted chat

A federated system is a client–server system in which anyone can start a server and the servers talk to each other; together they form the Fediverse.

91.7 What could be built

The advice is to avoid the harmful things, “but not to throw their phone into the ocean.” The map’s purple bits, “the bits we are building now”, aim at personal sovereignty “combined with a crazy, creative world of web apps anyone can dream up.” The note ends: “It is in fact possible.”

What happened since

  • Restrictions spread. Australia passed a law in November 2024 setting a minimum age of 16 for social media accounts, in force from 10 December 2025: the kind of withdrawal this note was written to complicate.

The short version

Life happens in groups of many sizes, but software offers only public and private. Buildings solve this with a sequence of rooms that can be told apart at a glance. Berners-Lee proposes doing the same for data in Solid, starting with colour, and designing the private end as carefully as the public one.

The question

At W3C, public pages are white, member-only pages pale blue and staff-only pages straw. Even so, people mailed non-public material to public lists: the pages carried the cue, but the window for writing a message did not. Berners-Lee’s 2022 note, closing Volume VII, starts from a building to ask why software offers only two settings:

If in our systems we cater for only the public and only the private extremes, we miss out most of our lives! Most of our lives involve doing things with combinations of different sized groups of people. The Intimacy Gradient, 2022

74.1 From the entrance to the private room

The note begins with a city and a house:

A city has public places where I can do all kinds of things, and also a private house with a private room which may be by myself. In that house there are spaces where I do things with family, friends, colleagues. The Intimacy Gradient, 2022

The architect Christopher Alexander’s A Pattern Language catalogues recurring design solutions; one, Intimacy Gradient, gives the note its title. It lays a building out as a sequence: from the entrance, through slightly more private areas, to the most private ones.

The web, he says, must do the same, “so I can easily recognize the difference, easily know which I am in, and easily welcome people to come into the more intimate parts, as I want.”

74.2 White, pale blue, straw

A simple way to start making an intimacy gradient on the web is with style. The Intimacy Gradient, 2022

The W3C colours of the opening, also used in the list archives, are first of all about safety:

For one thing, for security, anyone looking at a page will know who they can and cannot share things with. The Intimacy Gradient, 2022

To patch the mail gap, W3C started a convention: public list addresses contain the word “public-” as a warning. The note adds:

Mail clients don't color the background of a message you are composing as a function of how large the audience is your are addressing - bit perhaps they could! The Intimacy Gradient, 2022

74.3 Both ends of the gradient deserve design

The note’s real subject is Solid, where each person keeps their data in a pod: a personal store on the web that they control (Chapter 54). A pod holds public things such as a profile, private things such as settings and preferences, and things shared with groups.

The public end has been built. People want to be proud of a profile, as on LinkedIn or Instagram (“Remember myspace?”), so SolidOS lets a person set a highlight and background colour for theirs, usable wherever they appear. Then he turns the argument around:

However, thinking about both intimacy gradients and the different styles of the W3C web site, surely we should not stop there but make sure we think about the style of the private places too. The Intimacy Gradient, 2022

His instruction: make intimate places respect that intimacy by default, and let people control them.

A home, not a showroom

The private parts of a pod, he says, should feel like a home: the owner feels secure there and is in charge, filing by name or by date in a style of their own. Because Solid lets people choose their apps, they can also adopt a developer’s ideas of home.

74.4 Why this closes the volume

Across this volume, several systems allow too few settings: reactions reach one fixed audience (Chapter 64), only a certificate company can grant trust (Chapter 66), security is a yes-or-no in the address (Chapter 67), and the host name is the only line between parties (Chapter 68). The architect’s gradient names the missing middle.

74.5 What varies along the gradient

The note ends by asking what the gradient is a gradient in, naming four dimensions. The cells are this book’s illustration.

DimensionPublic endIntimate end
Colour and themeA clear warning that many can seeThe owner’s choice, restful
FontsThe community’s house styleWhatever the owner finds easy to read
Protection from mistakesConfirm before anything goes outFew interruptions
Fact-checkingClaims checked before they spreadNotes to oneself left as written

The third row returns to the opening: a public list is where a tool should ask twice. Because the gradient runs through all of Solid, everyone coding a piece of it must know where each interaction falls. The note’s first step is W3C’s again: pick up key colours from the user’s preferences and from their communities’. A system with only public and private offers the street and the locked room; people live in the rooms between.

What happened since

  • The composing window is still uncoloured. Microsoft’s MailTips, since Outlook and Exchange 2010, warn in a line of text when a message goes to outside recipients or a large list; the window itself looks the same.
  • Audience selection stayed coarse. Instagram’s Close Friends list (2018) added one extra audience beside followers; Mastodon offers four fixed visibilities (Chapter 64). Overlapping groups of different sizes remain hard to express.
